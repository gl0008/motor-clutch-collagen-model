The question

What decides how a tumor escapes its matrix?

A ~200 µm tumor organoid sits in reconstituted collagen-I. Over days it does three things we can measure in the wet lab:

  • Grips & pulls collagen — leader protrusions generate traction.
  • Reorganizes fibers into radial alignment (aster / TACS-3-like), with clearance zones.
  • Shows collective strands and occasional single-cell escape.

Goal: a mechanical phase diagram — given ECM mechanics + cell adhesion, predict the invasion mode.

Three modules, one coupled system. Each cell is a motor–clutch; the ECM is a discrete bead–spring network; the two talk through a Gaussian projection kernel (a representational tool, not a spring).

Clutches can slip (Bell unbinding) → traction load-and-fails, exactly as in single-cell motor-clutch theory — now scaled to a whole organoid.

Module 1 — the ECM itself

Collagen as a discrete bead–spring network

Fibers are chains of beads; crosslinks weld crossings. Every bead is overdamped (inertia-free) — velocity ∝ net force:

Overdamped bead dynamics
$$\zeta\,\frac{d\mathbf{r}_i}{dt}=\mathbf{F}^{\text{stretch}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{bend}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{xl}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{rep}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{active}}_i$$
Axial spring — tension stiff, compression soft (microbuckling)
$$\mathbf{F}^{\text{stretch}}=k_s\,(|\mathbf{r}_{ij}|-\ell_0)\,\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij},\quad k_s=\tfrac{EA}{\ell_0}\ \text{(tension)},\ \ k_s^{\text{comp}}=0.1\,\tfrac{EA}{\ell_0}$$
Harmonic bending + optional strain-stiffening
$$E_{\text{bend}}=\tfrac{\kappa_b}{2}\,\theta^2,\qquad k_s^{\text{tension}}\to k_s\,\exp\!\big(\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{\text{ref}}\big),\ \ \varepsilon=\tfrac{\Delta\ell}{\ell_0}$$
Baseline network: fibers (tension red / compression blue), crosslinks, boundary-anchored.

Module 2 — cell ↔ ECM (part 1 of 2)

The molecular clutch, with slippage

At each of 12 grip sites per cell, a motor pulls actin and clutches transmit that pull to a fiber. Fast clutches build force; when force is high they unbind faster (Bell) → the bond slips and traction resets.

Motor force–velocity (actin retrograde flow)
$$v=v_0\left(1-\frac{F}{F_{\text{stall}}}\right)$$
Clutch as a linear bond
$$F_c=k_c\,x_c$$
Bell's law — force-accelerated unbinding (slippage)
$$k_{\text{off}}=k_{\text{off}}^{0}\,\exp\!\big(F_c/F_b\big)$$

Biphasic traction–stiffness follows automatically: too soft → no load; too stiff → constant slipping; an optimum in between.

Module 2 — cell ↔ ECM (part 2 of 2)

Projecting the pull onto fibers

A grip site is a point on the cell surface; the fiber is made of discrete beads. A normalized Gaussian kernel spreads each clutch's point force onto nearby beads of the attached fiber — preserving total force and its first moment.

Gaussian projection weight (shell at cell radius R)
$$w_i=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\big(|\mathbf{r}_i-\mathbf{r}_c|-R\big)^2}{\sigma^2}\right)$$
Force on cell = reaction of traction on ECM (Newton III)
$$\zeta_{\text{cell}}\,\frac{d\mathbf{r}_c}{dt}=-\sum_i \mathbf{F}^{\text{active}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{cell-cell}}$$

important The kernel is a representational projection tool, not a mechanical crosslinker — it never adds elastic energy. (Hongbo-approved, 8/7.)

Before / after: the cell's pull reorganizes the near-field into a radial aster.

Module 3 — cell ↔ cell

Adhesion sets cohesion — the collective/single switch

Cells are soft adhesive disks. Below the equilibrium spacing they repel (volume exclusion); just above it they adhere (E-cadherin-like surface tension); far apart they feel nothing.

Piecewise-linear cell–cell force (separation d, spacing d₀)
$$f(d)=\begin{cases}-k_{\text{rep}}(d_0-d) & dd_0+\delta\end{cases}$$

This single parameter, k_adh, tunes tissue surface tension: high → cohesive pack (jammed), low → cells unjam and disperse.

Result 2 — the mechanical phase diagram

Adhesion × pull maps the regimes

Median cell displacement over adhesion (rows) × pull (cols).

Two axes reproduce the intuition:

  • High adhesion (≥40) → cells stay cohesive, barely move (~2–3 µm) = stuck at every pull.
  • Low adhesion + high pull → large displacement (50+ µm) but the pack breaks apart = dispersal / single-cell.
  • The collective strand lives in the narrow band between — enough cohesion to stay connected, enough pull to advance.

Result 3 — what makes it truly collective

A leader drags a connected strand

Homogeneous cells only expand radially or disperse. Heterogeneity (a few low-adhesion leaders) produces the real leader–follower strand: the leader runs ahead, followers advance behind it and stay connected.

Design logic

An ablation = flip one mechanism, compare one metric. Each row isolates a term in the force balance so we can attribute the phenotype, not just observe it.

Result 5 — matrix stiffness (in progress)

How soft is "too soft"? Does strain-stiffening bite?

Columns: modulus 1/3/10/30 MPa · rows: strain-stiffening off / ON.

Setup so far: baseline is deliberately soft (E = 3 MPa, softened from 32 MPa so the pull can visibly reorganize fibers).

  • At strong pull, soft matrix (E=1) → cells invade far (~119 µm) but the network is disrupted, not aligned (aster 0.76→0.67).
  • Strain-stiffening exists but sleeps: at current strains (~1–5% vs 6% ref) it barely engages — a known limitation, now being tested head-on.
  • Compression softening (microbuckling) is always on.

Honest gaps & next steps

What's missing — and what we test next

Not yet in the model

  • MMP proteolysis — cells cannot cut/degrade fibers, only pull them.
  • High-traction leaders — leaders differ only by adhesion, not force.
  • Proliferation / internal pressure in the organoid.
  • Viscoelasticity / plasticity persistence (SLS deferred; Bell-crosslink plasticity is opt-in).

On deck

  • Matrix stiffness × strain-stiffening sweep (running now).
  • Leader = low adhesion + high traction (Reffay 2014) — as a new, non-overlapping run.
  • Validate alignment rise (0.5→0.75) & displacement scaling against Saraswathibhatla / Kolade data.

caveat Swirling (Saraswathibhatla, breast) is not observed in Kolade's system — G5 does not assume it; radial alignment here is direct contraction.
