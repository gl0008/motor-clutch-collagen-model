# G4 v3 assumption ledger

Every assumption receives an identifier so later generations can state whether
they retain, calibrate or remove it.

| ID | Assumption | Why it is needed now | Expected impact | How it will be checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-001 | The first calibration is two-dimensional. | It isolates fibre rotation, bending, stretching and linking before 3D geometry is added. | Intersection frequency and long-range connectivity are not quantitatively transferable to 3D. | Treat only mechanism direction as informative; later repeat in 3D. |
| A-002 | Source-fibre angles are uniform on \([0,\pi)\). | The question requires alignment to be an output rather than a designed input. | A finite sample still has nonzero local order. | Report initial global nematic order and shell-wise \(S_r(0)\) for every seed. |
| A-003 | A circular void is cut before all cumulative stages. | It lets every stage reuse identical ECM topology when the cell is later activated. | The passive baseline already contains cell-sized geometry, although it has no cell force. | Label the early stages “inert void,” not “cell-free ECM.” |
| A-004 | Fibres are initially straight between sampled endpoints. | It provides a clean zero-rest-curvature mechanics test and preserves sampled orientation exactly. | It omits collagen crimp and could overestimate early tensile recruitment. | Add measured waviness only after the straight-network benchmark is understood. |
| A-005 | Outer-boundary endpoints are fixed. | A finite force-transmission problem otherwise contains rigid translation. | Far-field constraint can raise apparent network stiffness. | Later compare larger boxes and mobile-boundary control; do not interpret whole-network drift as remodeling. |
| A-006 | Benchmark intersections are all permanent freely hinged links. | This matches the rigid-link calibration logic used by Abhilash et al. and tests the elastic solver first. | It is more connected than a biological collagen network and may over-transmit force. | The biological stage replaces it with nested probabilistic links and reports connectivity. |
| A-007 | Inner-boundary contraction is prescribed at 5%, 10% and 20%. | It separates ECM mechanics from an uncalibrated active-force or clutch model. | It does not predict the force generated by a cell. | Record the reaction force; later ask whether the motor–clutch model can generate the same range. |
| A-008 | Overdamped drag is deterministic; Brownian force is omitted. | At the current micron-scale coarse graining, the target is cell-driven mean remodeling rather than thermal bead diffusion. | Short-time fluctuations are absent; drag controls approach time only. | Add noise only if an experiment to calibrate fluctuation amplitude is selected. |
| A-009 | \(E_R=E_{stretch}/E_{bend}\) is a deformation-mode diagnostic. | It tests the published bending-to-stretching recruitment mechanism. | \(E_R>1\) does not prove biological remodeling or permanent alignment. | Always report \(E_R\) together with \(\Delta S_r\), displacement and force. |
| A-010 | Dynamic contact relocation is not included in the benchmark. | No located paper directly validates the exact relocation rule proposed for this model. | Early results cannot address attachment following or migration. | Introduce it only in v3-E and compare against fixed material-point contact. |
| A-011 | A 2D geometric intersection is a candidate crosslink, retained when its fixed random mark is below \(p_x\). | It gives a controlled, nested connectivity series without changing fibre geometry. | 2D intersections are more frequent than true 3D contacts, so \(p_x\) is an effective connectivity parameter—not a crosslinker concentration. | Report candidate links, retained links, contact-connected fraction and boundary-connected fraction; later replace with a 3D proximity rule. |
| A-012 | Direct contact uses a hard 0–3 um surface band, followed by Gaussian weights with \(\sigma_c=1.5\) um. | It implements the advisor-approved hybrid without applying force to distant ECM. | Contact count depends on random local geometry, and these widths are not calibrated to one integrin/collagen experiment. | Report contact count, gaps and all weights; sweep only after a matching experiment is chosen. |
| A-013 | The fixed-traction check retains G4 v2 totals of 12, 24 and 48 nN. | It tests continuity with the previous generation without inventing a larger force to obtain a dramatic movie. | These are effective totals, not molecular motor forces, and may be insufficient to reach the prescribed-contraction benchmark. | Compare the resulting reaction/displacement with the benchmark; do not tune beyond the inherited range without a source. |
| A-014 | Contact material points remain fixed during v3-C. | It isolates deterministic force transmission before adding attachment turnover or relocation. | A deformed fibre can move away from the geometrically closest point while the model continues pulling the original material point. | Relocation remains a separate v3-E hypothesis, only after deterministic mechanics is accepted. |

## Decisions that remain open

- Crosslink probability is not yet calibrated to one collagen concentration.
- The mapping from 2D fibres per field to a 3D collagen density is not yet fixed.
- Straight-fibre bending rigidity, axial modulus and drag retain G4 v2
  mechanism-first values; they are not fitted to a specific tumor line.
- The number and lifetime of cell–collagen attachment sites will not be chosen
  until the deterministic traction stage passes its mechanics checks.
