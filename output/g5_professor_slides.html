The question

What decides how a tumor escapes its matrix?

A ~200 µm tumor organoid sits in collagen-I. Over days the wet lab sees it:

  • Grip & pull collagen — cells generate traction.
  • Reorganize fibers into radial alignment (aster / TACS-3-like).
  • Switch between collective strands and single-cell escape.

Goal — a mechanical phase diagram: given ECM mechanics + cell adhesion, predict the invasion mode.

Validation target: collagen alignment rises (~0.5→0.75) toward the invading front.
SOURCES Provenzano 2006 BMC Med (TACS) · Saraswathibhatla 2025 bioRxiv (alignment 0.5→0.75) · Adebowale/Liu wet-lab (PDAC & 4MOSC1 OSCC, pers. comm.)

Three modules — a motor–clutch cell, a bead–spring ECM, and a projection kernel between them — solved together. Clutches can slip (Bell unbinding), so traction load-and-fails exactly as in single-cell theory, now scaled to a whole organoid.

Theory · the ECM itself

Collagen = beads, springs, crosslinks

Fibers are bead chains; crosslinks weld crossings, so pulling one fiber drags its neighbors. Every bead is overdamped — velocity ∝ net force:

Overdamped bead dynamics
$$\zeta\,\frac{d\mathbf{r}_i}{dt}=\mathbf{F}^{\text{stretch}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{bend}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{xl}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{rep}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{active}}_i$$
Axial spring — tension stiff, compression soft (microbuckling)
$$\mathbf{F}^{\text{stretch}}=k_s\,(|\mathbf{r}_{ij}|-\ell_0)\,\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij},\qquad k_s^{\text{comp}}=0.1\,k_s$$
Harmonic bending
$$E_{\text{bend}}=\tfrac{\kappa_b}{2}\,\theta^2$$
Live: cells contract the matrix — watch a crosslink drag the fiber it's welded to. Red=tension, blue=compression, gold=crosslinks.
SOURCES Lee 2014 PLoS ONE · Saraswathibhatla 2025 SI Table 2 (k_s=4×10⁻³ N/m, κ_b=8.27×10⁻²⁰ N·m, ℓ₀=1 µm, k_xl=2×10⁻³ N/m)
Theory · cell ↔ ECM

The molecular clutch, with slippage

At each of 12 grip sites, a motor pulls actin and clutches transmit the pull to a fiber. Force builds; when it's high, clutches unbind faster (Bell) → the bond slips and traction resets.

Motor force–velocity (actin retrograde flow)
$$v=v_0\!\left(1-\tfrac{F}{F_{\text{stall}}}\right)$$
Clutch bond · Bell force-accelerated unbinding (slip)
$$F_c=k_c\,x_c,\qquad k_{\text{off}}=k_{\text{off}}^{0}\,\exp\!\big(F_c/F_b\big)$$

Biphasic traction–stiffness follows for free: too soft → no load; too stiff → constant slipping; optimum between. Emergent organoid traction ≈ 114 nN.

Live clutch dynamics (2 h): grip sites load, slip, and reel cells outward.
SOURCES Chan & Odde 2008 Science · Bell 1978 Science · Bangasser 2017 Nat Commun (biphasic) · Params: Adebowale 2021 Nat Mater SI Table 4 (k_c=2 nN/µm, k_on=0.055/s, k_off⁰=0.018/s, F_b=1.5 nN, v₀≈24 nm/s)
Theory · projection & cohesion

How the pull reaches fibers — and holds cells together

Cell → fiber. A normalized Gaussian kernel spreads each clutch's point force onto nearby beads of its fiber (a representational tool, not an elastic spring). The reaction reels the cell.

Gaussian projection weight (shell at cell radius R)
$$w_i=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(|\mathbf{r}_i-\mathbf{r}_c|-R)^2}{\sigma^2}\right),\qquad \zeta_{\text{cell}}\dot{\mathbf{r}}_c=-\!\sum_i \mathbf{F}^{\text{active}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{cc}}$$

Cell ↔ cell. Cells are soft adhesive disks: repel when overlapping, adhere (E-cadherin-like surface tension) just past contact. One knob, k_adh, sets tissue cohesion → the collective/single switch.

Piecewise-linear cell–cell force
$$f(d)=\begin{cases}-k_{\text{rep}}(d_0-d)&dd_0+\delta\end{cases}$$

Result 3 — the current focus

Matrix stiffness × strain-stiffening

Columns: modulus 1/3/10/30 MPa · rows: strain-stiffening off / ON.

Q1 — is 3 MPa too soft? At strong pull, very soft matrix (E=1) lets cells invade far (~119 µm) but scatters the network (aster 0.76→0.67). At E=3 the pull instead aligns fibers into an aster (0.76→0.79). Softness trades alignment for raw displacement.

Q2 — does strain-stiffening bite? Yes — strongest at the baseline: ON at E=3 gives the largest aster rise (+0.06 vs +0.03) and the tightest cohesion (1.25 vs 1.61). Taut radial fibers stiffen → transmit the pull → align the near-field and hold the pack together.
