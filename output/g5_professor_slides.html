The question

What decides how a tumor escapes its matrix?

A ~200 µm tumor organoid sits in collagen-I. Over days the wet lab sees it:

  • Grip & pull collagen — cells generate traction.
  • Reorganize fibers into radial alignment (aster / TACS-3-like).
  • Switch between collective strands and single-cell escape.

Goal — a mechanical phase diagram: given ECM mechanics + cell–cell adhesion, predict the invasion mode.

Theory 1/3 · the ECM itself

Collagen = beads, springs, crosslinks

Fibers are bead chains; crosslinks weld crossings, so pulling one fiber drags its neighbors. Every bead is overdamped — velocity ∝ net force:

Overdamped bead dynamics
$$\zeta\,\frac{d\mathbf{r}_i}{dt}=\mathbf{F}^{\text{stretch}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{bend}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{xl}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{rep}}_i+\mathbf{F}^{\text{active}}_i$$
Axial spring — tension stiff, compression soft (microbuckling)
$$\mathbf{F}^{\text{stretch}}=k_s\,(|\mathbf{r}_{ij}|-\ell_0)\,\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij},\qquad k_s^{\text{comp}}=0.1\,k_s$$
Harmonic bending
$$E_{\text{bend}}=\tfrac{\kappa_b}{2}\,\theta^2$$
Live: cells contract the matrix — watch a crosslink drag the fiber it's welded to. Red=tension, blue=compression, gold=crosslinks.

Theory 2/3 · cell ↔ ECM

The molecular clutch, with slippage

At 12 grip sites per cell, a motor pulls actin and clutches transmit the pull to a fiber; high force → faster unbinding (Bell) → the bond slips and traction resets.

Motor force–velocity · clutch bond · Bell slip
$$v=v_0\!\left(1-\tfrac{F}{F_{\text{stall}}}\right),\quad F_c=k_c\,x_c,\quad k_{\text{off}}=k_{\text{off}}^{0}\,e^{F_c/F_b}$$
Gaussian projection: clutch point-force → fiber beads (shell at R)
$$w_i=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(|\mathbf{r}_i-\mathbf{r}_c|-R)^2}{\sigma^2}\right)$$

Biphasic traction–stiffness follows for free. The kernel is a projection tool, not an elastic spring. Emergent organoid traction ≈ 114 nN.

Theory 3/3 · cell ↔ cell — the new G5 layer

Cell–cell adhesion: the collective ↔ single switch

This is what makes G5 multicellular. Each cell moves under the reaction of its own traction plus forces from its neighbors — the governing equation of the organoid:

Overdamped cell m (reaction + cell–cell)
$$\zeta_c\,\frac{d\mathbf{r}_{c,m}}{dt}=\mathbf{R}^{\text{ECM}}_m+\sum_{n\neq m}\mathbf{F}^{\text{cell-cell}}_{mn}$$
Cell–cell force: repel below spacing, adhere just above (E-cadherin)
$$f(d)=\begin{cases}-k_{\text{rep}}(d_0-d) & d\ \text{<}\ d_0\\[2pt] +k_{\text{adh}}(d-d_0) & d_0\le d\le d_0+\delta\\[2pt] 0 & d\ \text{>}\ d_0+\delta\end{cases}$$

One knob, k_adh, tunes tissue surface tension: high → cohesive pack (jammed); low → cells unjam and disperse. A subset with reduced k_adh = EMT/leader cells.

Same clutch physics, different k_adh & pull → stuck / collective / single-cell. Red = low-adhesion (EMT) cell.

Result 3 — matrix stiffness × strain-stiffening

Is 3 MPa too soft? Does stiffening bite?

Q1 — too soft? No. Sweeping E shows 3 MPa is the sweet spot: very soft (E=1) lets cells scatter and disrupt the network (aster 0.76→0.67); at E=3 the pull instead aligns fibers into an aster (0.76→0.79).

Q2 — strain-stiffening? Yes, strongest at E=3: turning it ON gives the largest aster rise (+0.06) and tightest cohesion (1.25). Taut radial fibers stiffen → transmit the pull → align & hold the pack.

$$k_s^{\text{tension}}\to k_s\,\exp\!\big(\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{\text{ref}}\big),\quad \varepsilon=\tfrac{\Delta\ell}{\ell_0}$$

Tensile stiffness rises with strain (compression stays soft). Reference ε_ref = 6 %.

Rigor — is the G4 → G5 step clean?

Isolating "multicellularity" from recalibration

Going 1 → M cells, several ECM/mobility parameters had quietly changed too. To attribute any effect to multicellularity, we freeze all non-multicell params to their G4 values and run transfer controls.

  • G5-0A · scale-transfer: 1 cell, all-G4 params, big box, matched density. Per-clutch physics transfers cleanly (per-sector 1.15×); the 0.29× total is purely geometric — a lone cell grips 3/12 sectors vs G4's forced 12.
  • G5-0B · cell-number-only: fixed cells, vary M, G4 params → the pure collective-traction effect Δmulticell = Y(M) − Y(1).

Fix identified: the widened contact / corona exist to restore near-cell fiber coverage; the clean fix is geometric, then contact width returns to G4's 3 µm.

G5-0A: single cell, G4-frozen. Total 0.29× G4 but per-sector 1.15× → physics clean, gap is geometric.
